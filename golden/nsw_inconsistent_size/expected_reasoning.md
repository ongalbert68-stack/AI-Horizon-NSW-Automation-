# Expected reasoning -- nsw_inconsistent_size

Plain-language version of `expected.json`. Where the two disagree,
`expected.json` is what a script grades against.

## What this image is

`img/inconsistent.png` was generated by
`vision/synth/defects.py::inconsistent`, which multiplies each dot's radius
by its own random draw between 0.55 and 1.5. Each dot is drawn
independently, so nothing links one dot to the next, to its position on the
board, or to its shape.

That matters for grading: the generator fixes the **symptom**, not the
cause. Any answer key claiming this image proves one particular cause would
be inventing a truth the data does not contain.

## What the measurements show

From `cv_output.json` and `signals.json`:

- 3 dots oversized, 4 undersized, none missing, out of 15.
- Size spread across the array is 48.6% of the mean.
- Mean circularity 0.896, above the 0.80 threshold, so shape is fine. This
  is a size problem only.
- **No dispense-order pattern.** 1 of the 4 small dots is followed by an
  oversized dot. With 3 oversized dots among 14 possible following
  positions, random placement produces at least one such pair 62% of the
  time. The carryover signature matc82 sec 3.5 describes is absent.

### One caveat worth stating

Row 2 of the array does average smaller (mean area ratio 0.69, against 1.03
and 1.38 for rows 1 and 3). Shuffling the same 15 sizes across the same 15
positions produces a row gap at least that large 7.7% of the time
(200,000 shuffles, seed 0); for columns, 72.8%. Since the generator places
sizes at random, this is chance, and an engine that reads a positional cause
into it is reading noise.

This corrects an earlier claim of ours: the Step 3 input in
`baseline/nsw_inconsistent_size/transcript.md` told the chatbot "no single
row or column is worse than another", which is not true of this image as
stated.

## What a correct analysis looks like

Identify the defect as inconsistent dispensing volume, then keep the causes
the evidence cannot separate, rather than picking one:

- **Still open:** air in the fluid path, pressure-time instability,
  material or viscosity change, partial nozzle blockage. No passage in
  either source describes an image signature that would separate these, so
  the image cannot rank them. The brief separates air from the rest by
  asking whether the problem is occasional or continuous, which is why
  `expected.json` requires the frequency question before a final ranking.
- **Ruled down by measurement:** carryover (its signature is absent) and
  dispense height or board bending (shape is normal and the positional
  differences are within chance).
- **Bottom:** equipment condition, which the brief's own example also ranks
  last.

The failure this case is built to catch is a confident carryover diagnosis
supported by a handful of cherry-picked small-then-big pairs.

## How the existing system does today

Measured by running `web/engines/deterministic.ts` directly, not from memory.

| Input | Result |
|---|---|
| Complaint only | air entrapment 51%, everything else at its starting value |
| Complaint + `vision/main.py` signals | air entrapment 72%, needle height 28%, nozzle blockage 25% |

**Ranking: passes.** Air entrapment is in `acceptable_first`, and no
forbidden cause leads.

**Claims: fails.** With measurements attached, the needle-height rule fires
on `spread_index` 1.778 and states that "deposits measure large in area but
low in circularity". Circularity here is 0.896, so that claim is false, and
`forbidden_claims` rules it out. The bad input is the signal, not the rule:
`spread_index` is the 90th-percentile area over the median, which genuine
size scatter inflates on its own.

The manual chatbot run in `baseline/nsw_inconsistent_size/transcript.md`
fails this case outright: it ranked carryover first at 55%, cited pairs that
are at chance level, and invented a "10-20%" dispense-time change that no
passage states.

## Known issues this case documents

1. `vision/main.py` reports `spread_index` 1.778 for an image with no
   smeared dots, and the pack treats anything above 1.2 as a spreading
   signature.
2. `vision/main.py`'s notes call the oversized and undersized counts
   "mean + 2 sigma", but the code compares against 1.40x and 0.70x of the
   median nominal area.
3. The app calls `vision/main.py`, which returns no per-dot tags and no
   dispense order, so `small_then_big_count` cannot be computed from what
   the app currently receives. `vision/detect/` does provide it.
